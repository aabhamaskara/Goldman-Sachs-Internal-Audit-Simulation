# Goldman Sachs Internal Audit Job Simulation (Forage)

## Overview
Completed Forage's Goldman Sachs Internal Audit job simulation, working through
three audit scenarios modeled on the firm's Global Banking & Markets (GBM)
division. Each scenario involved identifying a control weakness from data and
recommending a practical improvement — the core skillset behind internal audit
and GRC work.

## Scenario 1: Trade Execution — Fixed Income Trading Desk
7.5% of trades showed execution discrepancies, with average execution time
exceeding the industry standard and an estimated financial impact to the desk.

**Finding:** The issue could stem from either operational workflow gaps or
underlying technology performance — the two aren't mutually exclusive.

**Recommendation:** A Business Audit to evaluate trader procedures and
escalation practices, paired with a Tech Audit to assess system performance
and root cause, with the team that identifies the root cause leading
remediation.

## Scenario 2: Credit Risk Assessment — Corporate Lending
Non-performing loans (NPLs) rose year-over-year, concentrated in mid-sized
corporate borrowers relative to their share of the loan book.

**Finding:** The gap wasn't in loan approval — it was the lack of ongoing
monitoring after approval, so deterioration went undetected until default.

**Recommendation:** Early-warning indicators (covenant breaches, late
payments, credit score declines), scheduled reviews of higher-risk accounts,
mandatory borrower financial reporting, and defined escalation/restructuring
steps.

## Scenario 3: Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Controls
Suspicious transactions were being flagged, but a meaningful share were not
formally reported, and AML training coverage fell short of the internal
benchmark — a recurring issue from prior audits.

**Finding:** Detection wasn't the core problem; consistent follow-through was.

**Recommendation:** A mandatory case management workflow with sign-off on
every alert, automatic escalation of unresolved cases, compliance officer
review of closed-without-report cases, reporting rate tracked as a KPI, and
completion of AML training for all remaining staff.

## Key Takeaway
Across all three scenarios, the common thread was that risk isn't just about
detection — it's about whether there's an enforced process for what happens
after something is flagged. That's the lens I'm building toward a career in
internal audit and GRC: identify the gap, then design the control that forces
follow-through.
